# From visitor needs to actions

Use this method for content and opportunity review. Depth follows the audited scope; a small utility page may need only one row. Research does not require private accounts, paid tools, or invented keyword metrics.

## Build a need-to-page map

1. Establish audience, language, country when relevant, and useful outcome. Start with the offer, actual page content, supplied support questions, and existing report decisions. Distinguish observed questions from plausible needs inferred by the auditor.
2. Group questions by the outcome the visitor wants: understand, compare, choose, obtain, or use something, for example. Different phrasings can share one need; similar words can express different needs. Do not create a page per keyword variation.
3. Locate existing pages and relevant sections, including navigation and the path to the intended action. Read the answer before classifying coverage as met, partial, missing, or unknown. A promise in a heading is not evidence that the page answers it.
4. Where useful and accessible, inspect public search results and relevant competing answers. Record the exact query, engine, date, language/country and access limits. Treat results as a sample of available answers, not proof of demand volume, ranking causes, or a format to copy. If search is unavailable, finish with local evidence and label demand hypotheses.
5. Decide whether to preserve, improve an existing page, investigate a need, or create a distinct page. New content needs a distinct useful purpose and a concrete contribution the owner can actually supply. Check for duplicate coverage and the owner's earlier refusals before proposing it.

Record the map in the scope/opportunity part of the report or a linked appendix:

| Need / intent | Evidence and confidence | Language / market | Existing page or section | Coverage and specific gap | Decision / action ID |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <what the visitor needs to accomplish> | <actual question, page observation, search sample, or explicit hypothesis; evidence ID> | <known context or unknown> | <URL/section, none found in stated scope, or unknown> | <met/partial/missing/unknown and why> | <preserve/improve/investigate/create; active RSG ID if justified> |

Example: two supplied questions about offline use and airplane mode can map to the same existing help section. If it lacks the installation prerequisite, improve that answer once. A separately inferred need for enterprise procurement stays a hypothesis until the product and audience make it credible; it is not automatically a new landing page.

## Prioritize without inventing demand

- Connect each active action to the site's goal, affected audience, strength of evidence, useful contribution, effort/dependencies, and verification criterion. Carry **every justified action** into the common queue; a content map is not a second hidden backlog.
- Treat keyword volume and difficulty as unknown unless a named source actually supplies them with a period and market. Suggestions/autocomplete, competitor presence, and a question from one user do not establish volume. Advertising competition is not organic ranking difficulty. Do not silently use English/US data for another audience.
- Do not delete useful low-volume material, manufacture statistics/experience, or propose broad content production solely to occupy more search results. Several pages sharing a term do not establish harmful competition between them; inspect intent and any available performance evidence.
- Distinguish acceptance (the page accurately answers the question and supports the next step) from outcomes (reader success, qualified visits, installations, citations), which may remain unmeasured.

The focus on original value and audience needs is grounded in [Google's helpful-content guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) and [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). These sources do not establish demand or a future gain for a particular proposed page.
